2 · The IP disagreement, stated fairly

You said it plainly: anything you built for 1 Million Women Dance is going to be owned by 1 Million Women Dance. I agree with the principle entirely. The work is defining built for precisely enough that it protects you without describing something I'm not able to give.

Two honest constraints:

  • The platform isn't mine to assign. FlowBond is a live business with other clients running on the same infrastructure. Assigning it to 1MWD would breach those contracts.
  • A literal reading breaks in the other direction too. If 1MWD owns the generic video-upload pipeline because it happened to be written during our build, then no other product I ever work on can upload a video — and 1MWD ends up owning plumbing it has no use for and can't maintain.

So the line isn't philosophical. It's architectural.

3 · Three layers

Application layer 1MWD owns outright

The HEART points engine and its economy. The Dance-Her-Dance relay mechanic. The dream and cause data model. The heart-colour system. The campaign engine. Every screen, all front-end code, all UX, all brand and copy, the 1MWD database schema, and the admin tooling. Assigned permanently. Never licensed to anyone else, DANZ included.

Data layer 1MWD owns absolutely

Every woman, every profile, every dance, every relay, every engagement event, and everything derived from any of it. FlowBond processes on instruction and holds no rights of its own — including no right to use it in aggregate or anonymised form. Exportable at any time; returned within thirty days of any exit, for any reason, including non-payment.

Platform layer FlowBond owns · 1MWD licenses

Generic, multi-tenant infrastructure that exists independently of this project: identity and authentication, hosting and content delivery, the transcoding pipeline, the database engine, the AI agent framework, analytics plumbing, email and funnel infrastructure. Pre-existing or independently developed. Licensed, not assigned.

The test, applied to every commit Would this code exist if 1 Million Women Dance had never been imagined?
YES → platform layer, FlowBondNO → 1MWD, assigned

A test only helps if it's applied at the time. So: a shared IP register — one living sheet listing every module, which layer it sits in, who signed off, and when. Reviewed at each release. Any disagreement gets resolved the week the code is written, not years later in a data room with lawyers billing by the hour.

The four guarantees that make this safe for 1MWD

What you actually need isn't ownership of plumbing. It's certainty that 1MWD can never be held hostage and that a buyer is never blocked. These deliver exactly that:

GuaranteeWhat it does
Source escrowThe platform layer's source sits with a third-party agent, released to 1MWD automatically if FlowBond fails, materially breaches, changes control, or if Steph ceases to control FlowBond.
A priced buyout1MWD may buy a perpetual, irrevocable, transferable, sublicensable licence to the platform layer at any time, for a sum fixed now. Pre-agreeing the number means it can never become leverage during an acquisition.
Category exclusivityFlowBond will not serve any competing women's cause-driven dance platform for the term plus twenty-four months.
No data lock-inUnconditional export and return, already drafted into the services agreement.

With those in place, diligence finds a company that owns its product, its data, and its brand, and holds a secured, priced, escrowed licence to its infrastructure. That passes. What doesn't pass is an undocumented dependency on a founder's other company — which is what we're both trying to avoid.

Worth naming out loud

The licence is also why this MVP is affordable. Building the platform layer from scratch is a twelve-month project costing several hundred thousand dollars. 1MWD gets it at roughly half commercial rate, deferred entirely until $300,000 is raised. That's the trade: the plumbing stays FlowBond's, and 1MWD reaches the launch date for a fraction of what it would otherwise cost.

4 · DANZ — see Document 4

DANZ is a dance platform. 1MWD is a dance platform. That overlap is real and it is disclosed here deliberately, because learning it from a diligence questionnaire in eight months would damage trust in a way no clause repairs.

It has outgrown a section in this document. DANZ is a separate movement and brand with its own thesis — tokenised movement rewards, wearables, on-chain — that doesn't activate until 2030 and reaches for Dance On The Moon in 2035, and Steph is offering Vandana equity in it. That's a two-way relationship between two companies, so it has its own paper: Document 4, DANZ × 1MWD — Boundary & Cross-Equity.

The short version, which the rest of this document depends on:

  • Nothing 1MWD-specific ever crosses. The HEART engine, the relay mechanic, the dream model, the heart-colour system belong to 1MWD and cannot appear in DANZ — guaranteed by the ownership split in section 3, not by good intentions.
  • DANZ will not build a women's cause-mobilisation product. That category is 1MWD's.
  • Rights of first refusal run both ways on movement-reward technology, rather than a hard non-compete between two companies the same person controls.
  • No data crosses, in either direction.
  • 1MWD stays free of tokens — and DANZ being a separate entity is what keeps every token and on-chain question quarantined away from 1MWD's cap table and brand partnerships.

My preference is still that we say all of this to each other on the call before any of it becomes a clause. It's a relationship question first and a legal one second.

5 · Steph's two hats

Inside 1MWD

Head of Operations and Product. Reports to Vandana. Holds equity. Acts in 1MWD's interest.

Inside FlowBond

Founder and CEO. Vendor to 1MWD. Acts in FlowBond's interest.

Those two interests are aligned almost always and opposed occasionally — at renewal, at a price change, at termination. The handling is simple and should be written down: Steph recuses herself from any 1MWD-side decision about the FlowBond relationship. Vandana holds sole authority on 1MWD's side for renewing, amending, expanding, or terminating it. Disclosed in advance, approved in writing, minuted.

8 · Why this much paper

You've deliberately kept things at high-level agreements so far, including with two other people, and that was the right call while this was still forming. It stops being the right call the moment money arrives — two warm investors means a priced round, and a priced round means every one of those understandings gets read by someone whose job is to find the gaps.

Better we close them ourselves, now, while we're both excited and neither of us has anything to defend. The version of this that fails isn't the one where the product doesn't work. It's the one where in eighteen months there's a room full of lawyers and two women who used to be excited together.
