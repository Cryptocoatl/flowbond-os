Why this document exists

Vandana wrote: How can we both share more clearly what we are both bringing to the table is the question babe. And then: I've only asked Key and Rich to take a look at what's there but I don't think it's clear enough for them to contribute.

She is right, and the reason is structural. Documents 03, 04 and 05 are negotiating documents — one side's position, written to persuade the other. An advisor cannot contribute to a position paper. They can only agree or disagree with it.

This is not that. This document takes both sides' claims and puts them under the same standard of proof, in one place, with the method for checking each one written next to it. Where a number is verified, it says how to verify it. Where a number is only asserted, it says what would evidence it. Where a number is a guess, it shows the arithmetic so an experienced operator can disagree with the arithmetic rather than with the person.

A note on the tone of section 3 and section 6

Some of what follows reads like diligence pointed at Vandana. It is — and it is pointed equally at Steph, in the same words, in the next column. This is deliberate and it is protective of both. A contribution that is not documented cannot be defended as an equity claim. Every asset Vandana names here, once evidenced, becomes something her lawyer can put a number on and her cap table can honour. The questions in section 6 are the questions her own investors will ask in month three. Better they are asked here, by us, while they are still cheap to answer.

5 · What it means for investors that the tech is owned by 1MWD

Vandana's second question, and the most consequential one in this file: What it would technically mean for investors that any tech we build is owned by the company we decide. For now that's 1MWD.

There are three ways to do it. They are not degrees of commitment — they are different legal instruments with different consequences, and the difference shows up in diligence, in an exit, and in what Steph is contractually able to sign.

  • Assignment — 1MWD owns the code outright The copyright transfers. 1MWD's balance sheet carries the asset, no third party can touch it, and the company can be sold without anyone's consent. This is what investors want by default, and for the dance-specific work Steph has already agreed to it. The problem is only at the spine. The identity, payments and media code proposed for 1MWD is the same code already running 30+ other live products, some for paying clients under existing contracts. Assigning it would either breach those contracts or strand those clients. It is not a reluctance — it is not Steph's to give.
  • Exclusive licence in a field of use — 1MWD owns dance, and holds the spine exclusively for dance Everything dance-specific is assigned. The generic spine is licensed to 1MWD perpetually, irrevocably, royalty-free, and exclusively within dance — meaning FlowBond cannot license that spine to any other dance product, ever, including its own. Investors get exclusivity exactly where it matters, plus the three protections that make a licence bankable: source escrow, step-in rights if Steph or FlowBond fails, and a fixed-price buyout option so 1MWD can convert the licence to ownership at a number agreed today rather than negotiated under pressure later. This is the structure investors actually accept, and not as a concession — it is how nearly every company built on a founder's pre-existing platform is papered.
  • Protocol layer — FlowBond sits underneath as infrastructure FlowBond is to 1MWD what Stripe is to a shop or AWS is to an app: 1MWD owns its entire application layer, its brand, its content and all of its user data, and calls FlowBond for identity, payments and media. Cheapest and fastest to launch. The cost is a vendor dependency, which investors will price unless it is mitigated by the same escrow, step-in and buyout terms as model 2.
