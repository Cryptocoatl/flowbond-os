Document 4 proposed a boundary and a mutual right of first refusal between two companies. You read it and wrote six words: this will not work, it's a partnership on dance or not. You were right and Document 4 is withdrawn. This document replaces it.

5 · Package A, in eight clauses

This is a term sheet, not a contract. It is written so that lawyers draft something we have already agreed to, rather than drafting the argument itself at four hundred dollars an hour.

  1. The Field is dance participation, and it is defined once. Exactly as set out in section 3 — the inside list and the outside list, both of them, in the signed document. The fence does not move afterwards by interpretation. If we want to widen it later, we widen it in writing and we price it again.
  2. Everything inside the Field belongs to 1MWD, and DANZ folds in. The application layer, the data layer, the mechanics, the brand work, the move-to-earn design, the DANZ assets and the DANZ thesis. Assigned, not licensed. I do not build dance outside 1MWD for as long as I am inside it, plus twenty-four months. In return, 1MWD does not build outside the Field using my platform layer — the fence runs both ways or it is not a fence, it is a leash.
  3. The generic spine is licensed, not contributed — with escrow and a buyout at a price fixed today. Identity, hosting, transcoding, the database engine, the agent framework. These serve other clients and are not mine to assign without breaching their contracts. So: source escrow released automatically on failure, material breach, change of control, or if I cease to control FlowBond; a buyout at a number we fix now, while neither of us has leverage over the other; category exclusivity for the term plus twenty-four months; and no data lock-in, ever, including in a payment dispute. You are never held hostage. I never lose my company. FOR COUNSEL
  4. Dance to the Moon is mine, in writing. You conceded this on the call in your own words. Verbal concessions evaporate, so it goes in the document — and to be clear about what it is and is not: the concept and the name are mine, and if 1MWD wants to dance to the moon with me, I would be delighted. Your line about dancing around the sun for 1 Billion Hearts is yours, unreservedly.
  5. The equity lands where the value lands, and cannot be moved afterwards. Fifteen percent, fully diluted, as the ceiling you named. Of the entity that actually holds the dance business. If dance value sits in 1 Million Women Dance, the grant is there. If it sits in 1 Billion Hearts, the grant is there. And either way, an anti-migration covenant: dance assets, dance revenue, dance IP and dance contracts do not move to an affiliate I hold nothing in without my grant following them or being made whole. This is clause five because it is the one that decides whether any of the others mean anything. See section 6. FOR COUNSEL
  6. Salary starts now; the difference is deferred, not forgiven. Five thousand a month from signature, rising to seven thousand on the raise, both as you proposed on the call. The gap between five thousand and a market rate for the role accrues as a deferred balance, payable from the raise or convertible at the next financing price at my election. An unpaid runway of unknown length is not a co-founder arrangement; it is a loan from me to the company, and it should be recorded as one.
  7. The build is valued once. One hundred thousand dollars — deferred, or contributed. Never both, and never neither. This is where we have quietly been assuming different things: my documents treat the stack as a vendor deliverable at a founding rate, and you treat it as a co-founder's in-kind contribution against the equity. Both are legitimate. Only one can be true. So pick: (a) one hundred thousand accrues as a deferred payable, settled from the raise, and the equity is priced as if I had contributed nothing; or (b) it is contributed capital, credited on the cap table at signature, and no cash is ever owed for it. What cannot happen is that it counts as contribution when we discuss equity and as nothing at all when we discuss cash. FOR COUNSEL — see the tax note below
  8. Governance: one advisor each, and the tie-break is structural. Joint decision-making on brand and product, exactly as we discussed. You name an advisor, I name an advisor, and the two of them name a third if they deadlock. Where FlowBond's own commercial relationship with 1MWD is on the agenda — renewal, pricing, termination — I recuse myself entirely and you hold sole authority. Disclosed in advance, approved in writing, minuted.

The sequencing that holds it together

1MWD closes first, completely. The FlowBond percentage is settled afterwards, on its own paper, on a later date.

You said on the call that you want a percentage of FlowBond. I am open to that conversation and I would rather have it properly than as the last item on a long night. But it has to be separate, for the same reason Document 4 said the DANZ grant to you had to be separate: when two grants move at the same moment, every concession gets traded twice, and afterwards neither of us can tell which one bought which. Close the first thing. Then open the second.

Two things counsel must look at before either of us signs

Securities. Percentage-based compensation for introducing investors can constitute unregistered broker-dealer activity under Section 15(a) of the Exchange Act, with exposure to both payer and payee and rescission rights running to the investors. So investor-introduction commission is out, replaced by fundraising equity or a flat fee.

Cross-border tax. I am a Mexican tax resident taking an interest in a US entity. The instrument choice cannot be made without cross-border review, and the 83(b) election window is thirty days from grant with no extension available. Getting this wrong is expensive and unfixable.

7 · Where this favours me

I said I would mark my own bias. Here it is. Six places where this document is drafted in my interest, and what your lawyer will probably say about each.

One · I drew the fence

The Field in section 3 is my line, chosen by me, and I chose it where my other work survives. A neutral drafter would not necessarily have landed there. The four carve-outs by name are the clearest example: they are my projects, protected by name, in a document I wrote.

Two · One hundred thousand is my number

The build valuation in clause 7 is unaudited and self-assessed. Nobody independent has looked at the code and priced it. If you want it valued properly, name an assessor and I will accept theirs over mine — and I will accept it even if it comes in lower.

Three · Fixing the buyout price today favours me

I argue in clause 3 that fixing the number now protects you from being held hostage later. That is true. It is also true that it caps what you would otherwise have to pay in a competitive acquisition, and that I benefit from certainty because my downside is bounded. Both things are true at once.

Four · "Where the value lands" is my framing

Clause 5 is drafted so the grant follows value wherever it goes. That is protective for me and constraining on you — it limits how you may structure your own group of companies in future. It is the single most restrictive thing I am asking for, and I want it named as such rather than presented as a technicality.

Five · The MESO in section 4 is a persuasion device

Three options presented together tend to make the middle one look reasonable. I built the table, I chose what appears in it, and I labelled my preference. You should test B and C on their own merits and not as scenery around A.

Six · The two-way fence in clause 2 is new, and it is mine

You asked for exclusivity from me. I have written it as running in both directions. That is a fair structure, but you did not ask for it and it constrains 1MWD in ways your original proposal did not. Your lawyer should look at it hard.

And once more, because it is the point of this section: take this to your own lawyer and send me the invoice. I would rather pay for you to be well advised than win a clause because you were not.

9 · Why I am giving up my company

Because you are right that it is one thing or the other, and because a partnership where I am quietly building the same future next door is not a partnership — it is a conflict with a nicer name on it. If we are going to do this, I would rather do it completely.

What I am asking for in return is not more. It is only that the thing I am folding into lands somewhere real: a fence with a line in it, so that four other people's projects survive; a percentage attached to the entity where the value actually accrues; a licence rather than a confiscation of the spine that serves clients who are not you; and my company still standing on the other side of the deal.

The line from Document 3 has not changed and it is still the right frame for all of this. The version of this that fails is not the one where the product does not work. It is the one where, in eighteen months, there is a room full of lawyers and two women who used to be excited together.
