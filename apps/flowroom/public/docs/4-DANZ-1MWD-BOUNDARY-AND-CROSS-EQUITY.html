1 · Why this is its own document now

The earlier draft treated DANZ as a conflict to disclose and wall off. That was wrong, and it undersold what's actually happening.

DANZ isn't a competing product hiding in the corner. It's a separate movement and brand with its own thesis — tokenised rewards for movement, wearables, on-chain — that may one day be the layer 1MWD plugs into. Steph is also offering Vandana equity in it. That makes this a two-way relationship between two companies, not a disclosure paragraph inside a personal role agreement.

So it gets its own paper, its own terms, and its own signature line.

Both of you already agree that a coin is not the way for 1MWD right now. Worth writing down why, so the decision holds when someone inevitably suggests it again:

It keeps every token, wearable, and on-chain question quarantined in a different entity with a different cap table and a different regulator conversation. 1MWD stays clean, brand-safe, and boring in exactly the way that lets it sign Apple. Vandana should want DANZ separate.

4 · The overlap, stated honestly

The earlier draft claimed a clean split into different fields. That was too neat. The truth is closer to this: DANZ and 1MWD are different layers of the same future, not different markets. Both are about women and movement. Both could involve the same person dancing. If 1MWD ever adds wearables or on-chain rewards, that is DANZ's territory.

Pretending otherwise would produce a document that falls apart the first time reality touches it. So the structure isn't a wall between two fields. It's a defined interface between two companies, plus a pre-agreed right of first refusal in both directions.

5 · Five commitments, both ways

  1. 1MWD's application layer never crosses.The HEART engine, the Dance-Her-Dance relay, the dream model, the heart-colour system — owned by 1MWD, never appears in DANZ, ever. This is guaranteed by the ownership split in Document 3, not by good faith.
  2. DANZ does not launch a women's cause-mobilisation product.DANZ will not build or operate a product whose primary purpose is mobilising women around social causes through dance. That category belongs to 1MWD.
  3. DANZ is built to plug into 1MWD.This is the plan, not a hypothetical. When DANZ activates, its wearables, movement measurement, and reward systems are intended to be available to 1MWD as infrastructure — on most-favoured-customer terms, offered to 1MWD before any third party. 1MWD is always free to decline and build elsewhere.
  4. 1MWD offers DANZ the work first.The mirror. If 1MWD decides to build a movement-reward layer itself or with someone else, it tells DANZ first and gives it a fair chance. Neither company can be held up; each keeps the right to walk.
  5. No data ever crosses.No shared users, no shared accounts, no shared analytics, no cross-promotion without both boards agreeing in writing. Two companies, two databases, two consent regimes.
Why first refusal beats exclusivity

A hard non-compete between two companies the same person controls is close to unenforceable and reads badly in diligence. A right of first refusal is enforceable, is normal, and converts the overlap from a threat into a pipeline. It also means neither company can be held up — each keeps the right to walk.

6 · Equity for Vandana in DANZ

Steph is offering Vandana equity in danz.now, so she holds real ownership in the wearable and on-chain future rather than watching it from outside.

This is a genuinely good instinct. It permanently kills the you're competing with us fear, because after it Vandana profits either way. But it needs structuring properly or it creates two new problems.

A 2030 horizon breaks normal vesting — read this before picking a number

A standard three-year vest, started now, completes in 2029 — before DANZ has a product, a user, or a launch. Vandana would be fully vested in a shell, and there would be nothing left to align her to the work that actually matters. That's a bad outcome for both of you.

Two better options. Milestone vesting: tranches release on entity formation, first wearable integration, first product launch, first 1MWD integration. Or grant later: a binding side letter now promising the equity, with the grant itself made when DANZ activates. The second is cleaner and cheaper.

Don't form the DANZ entity yet just to do this

Incorporating now means five years of Delaware franchise tax, annual reports, registered agent fees, and filings for a company with no operations — plus a stale cap table and a stale valuation. A binding side letter committing the equity on formation is just as real to Vandana, costs almost nothing, and doesn't create five years of administrative debt.

This is a change from the earlier draft, which suggested forming both entities together. That made sense when DANZ was a 2027 project. At 2030 it doesn't.

Sequence this carefully — it matters

If Vandana receives DANZ equity at the same moment she accepts a FlowBond-favourable IP position, it can look like the shares bought the concession. That's damaging to both of you: it undermines the IP agreement in diligence and it puts Vandana in an awkward position with her own board and investors.

Two fixes, both easy. Sign the 1MWD agreements first and close them completely. Then paper the DANZ grant separately, on a later date, in its own document, with a recital stating plainly that it is independent consideration for an advisory role and is not connected to the 1MWD arrangements. Have both counsels confirm the framing.

Also for counsel

An equity grant to a non-employee has tax consequences on receipt or vesting depending on the instrument and jurisdiction. Vandana should get her own advice before accepting — not Steph's counsel, hers. Offer to cover the cost of that review; it's a small amount of money and a large amount of trust.
