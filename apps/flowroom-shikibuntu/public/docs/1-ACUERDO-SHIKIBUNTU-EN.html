Shikibuntu · Gala & Steph ESEN

An alliance between friends · Draft for signature

We always dreamed of changing the world together. This is how we're going to build it.

You bring the human part. I bring the technology that holds it up. Shikibuntu is yours, and I help it exist.

Gala,

We've spent years dreaming this out loud. This document doesn't turn the dream into a contract: it turns it into a plan, so that both of us know exactly what each is putting in and what happens if the world moves.

I'm writing it now, while we're good, because friendships that break over a project almost never break from bad faith. They break because nobody wrote down the obvious in time, and later each person remembered it differently.

Two things are true at once, and neither swallows the other: Shikibuntu is yours, entirely. And we are building it together. Everything that follows exists to hold both truths at the same time.

01

What we are building

Shikibuntu is your movement: protecting the planet and all life, and giving people a concrete way into that work. I am because we all are. Because the Earth lives.

What we are building together is the body that movement needs in order to exist in the world: a place where people arrive, understand, stay and do something. With enough technology that it can grow without you having to ask anyone's permission — not even mine.

We have all the strength to create this. You know how to move people; I know how to build the ground they stand on.

02

What each of us brings

We're not splitting chores: we're putting two different kinds of magic on the same table. Neither one works alone.

Gala

The human part
  • The vision, the voice, and the judgement of what is and isn't Shikibuntu.
  • The community: who comes in, how they're received, how they're accompanied.
  • The real onboarding of people — the moment someone goes from watching to taking part.
  • The alliances, the allies, and the conversations only you can have.
  • The conservation work that makes everything else true.

Steph

The technology behind it
  • The whole platform, and whatever else needs building later.
  • That you can publish, translate, open sections and grant access on your own.
  • The infrastructure: hosting, data, security, backups and rollback.
  • The vehicles for moving money when it's needed: payments, donations and splits.
  • That none of this requires you to learn to be an engineer.

The practical rule: if the decision is about what Shikibuntu says or is, it's yours. If it's about how it gets built, it's mine. And when something falls in between — and it will — we talk about it before doing it, not after.

03

Who Shikibuntu belongs to

Building it together takes not one gram of ownership away from anyone. I'm writing this without nuance so there's no way to read it any other way:

Shikibuntu is yours. 100%, from today and permanently.

  • The name, the logo, the palette, the Earthkeeper Wheel, and everything that is the brand.
  • All the content: the writing, the translations, the images, the sections, the missions.
  • The data: your people, your wishes, your records, your log. None of it is used for anything else or shared with anyone.
  • Your audience and your relationship with them. There is no mailing list of mine in the middle.
  • The domain, once you move it into your name — section 7 says how.

Building with you doesn't make me the owner of anything of yours. It makes me your ally.

The one door that can open, and only if we both open it

I'm building other projects on the same infrastructure — ReciproCiudad, Sargazo, Moon Temple and several more. One day it may make perfect sense for Shikibuntu to ally with one of them: sharing a campaign, one funding the other's missions, joining forces for something big.

That never happens by default. No alliance, stake or crossover between Shikibuntu and another project of mine exists until we both decide it together and put it in writing: project by project, saying what each side puts in, what each side gets, and when it ends. Nothing retroactive, nothing automatic, and either of us can end it.

Document 02 has the list of those projects and what each could do for Shikibuntu. It's a menu, not a commitment: nothing there is signed.

04

The technology: what exists and what you receive

Already built and running: the site in two languages, your command panel, the content engine that lets you publish without asking anyone, the language system, the crew system, image uploads, the research room, the audit log, and versioned backups with rollback.

What you can do without writing to me

  • Change any text on the site and publish it. Before publishing, the system checks you haven't left a blank page or a broken link.
  • Add languages. Type a two-letter code and it translates everything, leaving proper names alone.
  • Add, hide or reorder sections from nine ready-made types: full freedom of content, zero risk to the design.
  • Roll back. Every publication saves a full snapshot; if you don't like something, one click brings it back.
  • Give access to your people, with six roles and nine permissions you tick one by one.
  • Read the log: who did what and when. Including me.

Who owns the code

What is Shikibuntu's is yours. All the code built specifically for you — your site, your panel, your templates, your configuration — is yours outright. You can use it, change it, take it, or hire anyone you like to work on it.

Underneath there is a base platform I built earlier, which other projects of mine also use. That one I can't assign to you because it isn't only mine to give, and assigning it would entangle you with work that has nothing to do with you. What I do give you is a perpetual, free, worldwide, irrevocable licence to use it for Shikibuntu, unconditionally, and I can never withdraw it — not me, and not anyone who buys my work later.

Nobody can switch Shikibuntu off on you. Not me angry, not me broke, not a company that someday buys FlowBond.

05

Your shield when someone proposes an alliance

Alliances are your court: who yes and who no is yours to decide. But nobody should have to read a contract written by the other side's lawyer alone, and more of them are coming your way.

What exists today: the research room in your panel. You give it the name of whoever is proposing something and it investigates in rounds, with real search, and leaves you the file saved for when you need it.

What I'll build when you need it — and it's one of the things I most want to give you:

  • You upload the proposal. The PDF they sent, or the text pasted as is. You don't have to summarise or understand it first.
  • ClaudIA translates it into plain language: what they're actually asking for, what you'd be giving up without it saying so in those words, what's missing that should be there, which clause ties you down and for how long.
  • It tells you what to ask back, in sentences you can copy and send.
  • It helps you draft your reply or counter-proposal. You read it, change it, approve it — and only then does it go out. Nothing is ever sent on its own.
  • Round after round, until the contract reads exactly the way you want it and not the way it was handed to you.
And what this is not

ClaudIA is not a lawyer and doesn't replace one. What it does is make sure you never sign something you didn't understand, and that when you do go to a lawyer you arrive knowing exactly what to ask. When the deal is big, a real lawyer gets paid — and that's a cost of the deal, not yours.

06

The vehicles for moving money

When Shikibuntu needs to receive money — donations, memberships, an event, a campaign, a sponsorship — we won't start from zero, and we won't depend on a platform that keeps a slice of it along with your data.

What I can put in place, when you need it and not before:

  • Payments and donations connected to your own account, with the money landing directly wherever you say.
  • Automatic, transparent splits: if something is divided between several people or causes, it splits itself and everyone can see how. Already built and tested.
  • Receipts and records of what comes in, so that accounting to a donor or a sponsor means opening a screen, not assembling a spreadsheet.
The money rule

Everything comes in under Shikibuntu's name and into your accounts. I build the plumbing; the money never passes through my hands.

07

Where it lives today, and how it becomes entirely yours

I'll say it plainly: today Shikibuntu lives in my accounts. The hosting is in my Cloudflare and the database in a project of mine shared with other things. It was the fast way to start, and it's what has to change for "it's yours" to be literal.

The plan, with no deadline and at your pace:

  1. You open your own accounts for hosting and database. They're free at the size we're at. I'll sit with you the first time.
  2. The domain in your name, registered and paid by you, even if it's a few dollars a year. It's the piece that makes it impossible for anyone to lock you out.
  3. I move Shikibuntu there and keep only the access you decide to give me, no more and no less.
  4. Your data leaves with you in a file you can read without me and without my software.
In the meantime

Even if this never happens, you have the right to ask me for a complete copy of everything — code, content and data — whenever you want, without explaining why. I commit to delivering it within seven days.

08

You set the share

Today you owe me nothing and I charge nothing. But if this grows and real money starts coming in, I don't want it to be awkward to talk about — so I'm writing it down now, with the number in your hands.

My share, if there's ever anything to share ___ %

You set the number, when there is something to share and not before.

So that day is an easy conversation and not a negotiation, this is the only thing we agree in advance:

  • When it's decided: the first time Shikibuntu receives real money. Not before, and never retroactively for what's already built.
  • On what: on what is left after Shikibuntu's costs are covered — never on donations earmarked for a specific purpose, nor on money that already has an owner.
  • How: we write it on a separate page and both sign it. If you prefer, the system splits it automatically and in the open, so nobody has to remember anything.
  • It can be revisited: once a year, either of us can say "this no longer reflects what we're each putting in" and we talk again. No drama, and nobody has to justify herself.
  • Zero is a valid answer. If one day you decide nothing is due, I understand and nothing changes: I keep building all the same, and Shikibuntu stays yours all the same.

I'm leaving it this way on purpose. I don't want a number negotiated between us today, when there's nothing to share yet and all it would create is pressure. I want it to come from you, when you see what this became.

The one thing that really does cost: credits

Hosting is free at this size and my work isn't charged for. What does get consumed every time it's used are generation credits: every video, every image, every voice, every long answer from ClaudIA. Cents per piece — but a thousand pieces are no longer cents.

That's where I don't want us to end up somewhere ugly: not you putting in money you don't have, and not me quietly absorbing it until it turns into a grievance. So I'm saying it now:

The credit rule

It doesn't come out of our pockets: we go and get it. We go looking for sponsors, donors and grants for a Shikibuntu credit fund — earmarked money that can only be spent generating things for your people, with an open ledger of what came in and where it went.

If there's no fund, we do less — never debt. A thousand times better to slow down for a month than to invent a cost that later sits between us.

No sponsor buys a say. Whoever puts in money funds missions and rewards; they don't edit, don't approve, don't weigh in on what Shikibuntu says, and don't see your people's data. If a sponsorship arrives with conditions about the content, it isn't sponsorship and we don't take it.

How that fund gets raised, what each reward costs and who might fund it — all of that is in document 02, with numbers.

10

The clause that matters most to me

If I had to delete this whole document and keep one paragraph, it would be this one.

Our friendship and Shikibuntu are two separate things, and neither one can take the other hostage.

Both directions, and I mean both:

  • If one day you and I fight, drift apart, or life simply takes us different ways, Shikibuntu is untouched. It stays yours, it stays running, and I hand everything over unconditionally. There is nothing here I could ever use to hurt you.
  • If one day Shikibuntu stalls, fails, disappoints you, or you decide to close it, nothing happens to us. You don't owe me a successful project in exchange for my love, and I don't owe you one in exchange for yours.

Neither of us will use this project as an argument in a personal conversation, or our friendship as an argument in a project decision. If something starts to feel tangled, we say so out loud before it knots.

11

What we ask of each other

An alliance holds what both of us hold up. This is what each commits to, and it's short on purpose:

  • Say when something isn't working, even if it feels awkward or trivial. A thousand times better to fix it than to find out you'd been putting up with it for months.
  • Say when you can't anymore. Neither of us promises infinite energy. If one of us runs out — of time, of will, of life — she says so in advance, leaves her part running, and helps someone else pick it up. That isn't abandoning: that's taking care of each other.
  • Don't decide for the other in her own court. I don't decide what Shikibuntu says; you don't decide how it's built. What falls in between, we talk about.
  • Use the panel. I built it so Shikibuntu moves at your pace and not mine. Every time you publish something without writing to me, this worked.
  • Let me say I built it, in my portfolio, without exposing anything of yours you'd rather keep private. If one day you'd prefer I didn't, say so and I'll take it down without argument.
12

A quarter of the year together

Of everything I've written, this is the only thing I ask of you as a condition. One, and I want to explain why it's this and not something else.

The only condition ¼

A quarter of the year, minimum, together.

Three months a year, added up — not in one stretch. Scattered weeks, one long season, work trips, or living in the same place for a while. What counts is the total.

Why this, and not a percentage, a target or a deliverable:

  • Shikibuntu doesn't get built over messages. At a distance I build what I imagine you need. With you in front of me I build what you actually need, and they aren't the same thing.
  • What falls "in between" gets solved in an afternoon and rots in a chat. Section 02 says the in-between gets talked through beforehand; this is where that talking happens.
  • It's what refills me. I don't charge and I don't want to, and being with you is literally where the energy to keep going comes from. I'd rather ask you for time than for money.
And this conditions nothing of yours

Let this be crystal clear, because "condition" is a heavy word: if one year it isn't possible, you lose absolutely nothing. Not the ownership, not the perpetual licence, not the site, not your data, not the right to have everything handed over in seven days. Sections 03, 04 and 13 don't depend on this or on anything.

The only thing that changes if we don't see each other is how deep in the building I am. And that gets said out loud, not charged for and not punished.

And the first one already has a date

Europe, next week. That's where the first quarter starts counting — and that's where we sign this, on paper and in person.

13

If one day each of us goes her own way

Let this be written while we're friends, not once we aren't. If either of us wants to end this collaboration, for any reason and without having to explain it:

  1. We say so. A message is enough.
  2. Within seven days I hand you everything: code, content, data, domains and access.
  3. For thirty days I stay available so whoever you bring in can understand it. At no cost.
  4. I delete my access to your systems and confirm it to you in writing.
  5. I keep no copy of your data, your content, or your list of people.
No conditions

None of this is conditional on the parting being friendly. It holds even if we're angry. And if we had already agreed a share, what was generated up to that day is honoured; from then on, nothing.

14

What this document is not

So that nobody — not a lawyer, not a sponsor, not the two of us in five years — reads something here we didn't say:

  • It is not a company or a joint venture. We are two people building together, not an entity.
  • It is not employment. I'm not your employee and you're not my client.
  • It gives me no stake, shares or rights over Shikibuntu. The share in section 8, if there is one, is about money — never about ownership.
  • It does not oblige you to use my technology: you can leave with someone else whenever you like.
  • It is not exclusive: I keep working on my other projects, and none of them touches Shikibuntu or your data.

On the legal side

It's written in plain language on purpose, so the two of us understand it without an interpreter. If at any point you'd like a lawyer to review it or make it enforceable where you live, that's completely fine and I'm not offended in the slightest — on the contrary, I'll pay for it.

I am because we all are. Because the Earth lives.
